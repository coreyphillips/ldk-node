# Native primary wallet client

The local `feat/lfbw-wallet-client` branch, based on `chicory-primary-compatibility` at `b63959d535fd66995b3c89213e102aa4f6d5f327`, adds the Beignet JIT receive client to LDK Node. It keeps the existing LDK node identity, payment store, channel monitors and onchain wallet. A primary is an authenticated Lightning peer; no wallet seed or signing key is sent to an HTTP service.

An application selects a peer with `Builder.set_primary_node`, connects its address using `Node.connect`, and checks the protocol through `Node.check_primary_connection`. Selecting a primary explicitly trusts its unconfirmed funding. Pairing sends a read-only one-satoshi quote with zero requested inbound headroom and accepts a valid correlated refusal as proof of protocol support. It does not require provider funds or ready channels, reserve liquidity, authorize a payment, or promise that a receive will succeed. The primary must support the Beignet custom peer protocol. Actual receive quotes and invoices still require its JIT service to accept the requested amount and all existing liquidity, channel-readiness and fee checks. Both a Beignet primary and the native Chicory-compatible LDK provider implement that protocol. Beignet additionally requires local branch `fix/jit-skim-fee-tlv`, based on `chicory-initial-commitment-fees` at `cc473ff08cd37cd5d0c4e60dae5032ffc34d079b`. It announces the agreed withholding in the type 65537 `update_add_htlc` TLV and persists those exact retransmission bytes. Without that change, LDK correctly rejects a skimmed receive whose onion still specifies the original amount. The Beignet branch also sends its signed private-channel forwarding policy when a channel becomes ready or reestablishes. Existing-channel invoice hints need that policy to include the provider's ordinary forwarding fee before its JIT splice decision. An ordinary Lightning node that ignores these requests times out instead of producing an unusable invoice.

`Node.receive_via_primary` authorizes a fixed amount and requires an explicit maximum fee in millisatoshis. Invoice expiry is capped at ten minutes to match provider authorization lifetimes. The request is bound to the invoice payment hash, the selected peer and a random correlation ID. The payment secret commits to the original invoice amount, so an intermediary cannot replace the onion total with a smaller payment; authorized skimming only reduces the delivered HTLC amount. Requests have a 15-second timeout and a bounded queue. A declined authorization, absent intercept SCID, fee mode mismatch or excessive fee produces no invoice. The signed invoice and exact allowed receiver fee are persisted as a normal LDK JIT payment before returning. Existing inbound aliases are used when a home channel already exists, together with that channel's advertised base fee, proportional fee and CLTV policy. This lets a payment reach the provider's splice decision instead of failing its ordinary forwarding checks. An existing ready channel without an advertised policy cannot produce a receive invoice yet. Otherwise the primary supplies a zero-routing-fee intercept route for its first channel. The existing claim handler rejects any withholding above the accepted allowance.

Quote and authorization failures distinguish `LiquiditySourceInsufficientFunds`, `LiquidityRequestRejected`, `LiquidityRequestTimedOut` and `LiquidityReceiveAmountTooSmall`. Insufficient funding matches the known native and Beignet refusal messages exactly; provider funding budgets, busy operations and unknown refusal text remain general rejections. An opening fee equal to or greater than the receive amount fails before invoice creation. Provider refusal text is recorded only as a diagnostic, limited to 200 characters with control characters escaped, without authorization payloads or payment secrets. Applications can display actionable messages from the typed error without exposing arbitrary provider text. These errors do not change the pairing probe, authenticated response correlation, funding requirements, amount limits or fee ceiling.

The primary-specific channel policy accepts zero-confirmation channels and bounded receiver fee withholding, and keeps stable funding keys across private-channel splices for Beignet compatibility. New private channels with the explicitly selected primary negotiate an aggregate inbound HTLC ceiling of `MAX_VALUE_MSAT` (the Bitcoin monetary range), rather than fixing it to the opening capacity. This permits a small deposit-funded channel to grow through later JIT splices without imposing a second lifetime receive ceiling. Live funding value, balances, reserves, commitment fees, dust exposure, HTLC count and payment-fee checks remain unchanged. Only these primary channels opt into retaining a peer's larger aggregate limit, and an explicitly enabled native JIT provider negotiates the same inbound policy for private channels when splice JIT is enabled. This supports Lightning sends above the original opening capacity after a receive grows the channel. Public channels and non-primary handshake defaults retain the prior policy.

Ordinary onchain deposits, channel opening, splice-in, Lightning sends and splice-out continue to use the native LDK APIs. `Node.has_pending_funding_operation` exposes the native funding guard for application restart reconciliation. `Node.estimated_fee_rates` reads the configured chain backend and does not contact an LSP. `Node.splice_out_with_fee_rate` uses the application-selected sat/vB rate. `Node.fetch_transaction_details` fetches external splice outputs and parent transactions from the configured backend and verifies each transaction ID. It proves transaction contents, not confirmation or wallet ownership; its net wallet amount is zero for unknown transactions.

`Node.fetch_transaction_status` independently queries the configured backend for external transaction confirmation, verifies inclusion evidence and returns the active block hash, height and timestamp. It returns unconfirmed only when the backend positively identifies the transaction as unconfirmed. Missing transactions, inconsistent evidence and backend failures return an error, so applications must preserve their prior activity state on those errors.

`Node.open_channel_with_user_channel_id` accepts the decimal u128 ID the application persists before dispatch. Native storage reserves supplied IDs before connecting or creating a channel, retains them after failure or closure, and refuses their reuse after restart. A reservation storage failure prevents dispatch. A deliberately new attempt must use a fresh ID after reconciling the prior one. This avoids a crash between channel creation and application bookkeeping leaving an unidentified operation.

Primary channel opens are checked before submission to LDK: all selected inputs must be confirmed and the actual transaction fee must be at most five percent of the total contribution. Primary splice-ins apply the same threshold to the confirmed coin-selection PSBT before negotiation. That splice check is a preflight policy, not a guarantee against every subsequent peer negotiation. Applications must preserve pending operation state and show estimated fees accurately.

Quote and receive requests refuse any selected-primary channel that is not yet ready, even if another primary channel is ready. This prevents a pending channel from triggering a second intercept channel; pending channels with unrelated peers do not block receiving. Existing channels keep the limits they originally negotiated, including after restart or selecting a primary later. A splice cannot renegotiate them. Quotes and invoice creation refuse a net delivered amount above that persisted limit with `InvalidAmount`; missing limit metadata fails closed. To migrate a pre-update primary channel, explicitly close it, wait for its funds to become available, then open a new private channel using this version. Do not edit channel state or replace a wallet seed to change this limit.

The client currently exposes fixed-amount JIT invoices and native TCP peer connectivity. FFOR offline receiving is not supported. Direct-funding request envelopes, Iroh transport and a generic Tor adapter are separate capabilities and are not implied by successful primary pairing.

## Local Android build

Run the binding launcher with its `android` target. It builds armeabi-v7a, arm64-v8a and x86_64 native libraries and regenerates their matching Kotlin bindings. Set `JAVA_HOME` to a Java 17 JDK, `ANDROID_HOME` to the installed SDK and `ANDROID_NDK_ROOT` to its installed NDK when those tools are not on the default path.

The generated local AAR is `bindings/kotlin/ldk-node-android/lib/build/outputs/aar/lib-release.aar`. Bitkit Android's local primary-node branch consumes that file through its `ldkNodeAar` Gradle property. Published LDK Node releases do not contain these local APIs. The shared UniFFI definition is updated; regenerate the other platform bindings before using the new interface on another platform.

## Validation

The library suite with the `chicory` feature exercises both provider and client paths. Tests cover exact authenticated response correlation, bounded queues, cancellation cleanup, disconnect cleanup, authorization cleanup and integer fee arithmetic, including property cases. Pairing regressions connect an unfunded native provider over loopback BOLT 8, verify that pairing succeeds while actual quotes and invoices remain refused with a funding error, and reject malformed, unsupported, wrong-peer and uncorrelated probe replies. Quote acceptance flags must be encoded as zero or one. Protocol proof retains the same 15-second response timeout and releases timed-out requests with a distinct timeout error. Refusal tests cover both providers' funding messages, unknown and policy rejections, fee-consuming amounts, and bounded escaped diagnostics including property cases. Additional tests reject a smaller onion total despite a valid payment secret, preserve caller-supplied open identity through independent SQLite access, reject consumed IDs, and fail closed on reservation storage errors. Network fixture tests require permission to bind local loopback sockets.

The `chicory-primary` regtest driver also accepts `LFBW_PRIMARY_URI` to run as a primary wallet client instead of a provider. Its read-only `check_primary` command tests pairing independently of provider funding. The `quote_primary` and `receive_primary` commands use the production native receive APIs and continue to reject declined requests. Its other commands allow a disposable test harness to exercise sends, confirmed deposits, splices, payment status and restart without introducing a production administration endpoint.

The following commands completed on the local branches:

```sh
cargo check --all-targets --features 'chicory uniffi' --locked
cargo test --lib --features chicory --locked
./bindgen.sh android
```

The native library suite passed 236 tests after adding the pairing and receive-error regression checks. The targeted pairing changes also passed `cargo check --lib --features 'chicory uniffi' --locked`. The focused vendored channel suite passed 23 tests and its channel-details serialization regression passed. Android packaging passed with Java 17 and NDK 28.2.13676358. Clippy with the installed Rust 1.99 toolchain is blocked by seven existing deny-level `erasing_op` and `never_loop` findings in vendored Lightning. These are not reported as a clean lint run.

Build the regtest driver and run the checked-in harness against an existing disposable chain. Set `LFBW_REGTEST_RPC_URL` to its `rpc://user:password@host:port` endpoint. The script verifies the chain is regtest before sending funds. It creates a disposable RPC wallet, draws 0.03 test BTC from `--faucet-wallet` (default `default`), creates fresh node stores, mines confirmations, then stops its nodes and unloads its RPC wallet. It leaves the node directories and a result JSON for inspection. Run one fixture at a time because mining advances the shared test chain.

```sh
cargo build --example chicory-primary --features chicory --locked
python3 scripts/primary-wallet-regtest.py --provider ldk
python3 scripts/primary-wallet-regtest.py --provider beignet --beignet-root /path/to/beignet
python3 scripts/primary-wallet-regtest.py --provider beignet --beignet-root /path/to/beignet --client-chain-url tcp://127.0.0.1:60001
```

Build the patched Beignet branch first with `npm run build` and make Node.js available on `PATH`. Its provider adapter uses `--electrum-url`, default `tcp://127.0.0.1:60001`. It persists its disposable regtest mnemonic in an owner-readable file before startup so the provider retains its identity on restart; an older store without that seed must be replaced with a fresh test store. The 226 focused Beignet wire, JIT manager, node forwarding and private-policy tests pass, including exact fee TLV encoding, aggregation and queued retransmission content.

All three funded fixtures passed: 20,000-sat invoice with a 120-sat fee quote, refusal of a lower fee ceiling, net receipt of 19,880 sats, 1,000-sat Lightning send, confirmed deposit and 40,000-sat splice-in, external 5,000-sat splice-out at the requested 2 sat/vB rate, transaction and parent lookups, restart with the same balances, and supplied channel ID propagation. The updated harness also opens a channel from a fresh wallet's confirmed deposit, restarts that wallet, and receives a 100,000-sat invoice (99,800 sats after its authorized fee) through a JIT splice into that same 48,200-sat channel, sends 60,000 sats back over Lightning, verifies mempool and mined confirmation status against the backend including after restart, and refuses an unknown transaction status. It restarts each provider with the same identity and creates another large invoice after reestablishment. A separate channel opened without the primary policy retains its old aggregate limit after restart, and both its oversized quote and invoice requests are refused. Finally it restarts its sender and refuses replay of both an active open ID and a consumed ID that never created a channel. Sanitized results are saved in [qualification/primary-wallet-client.json](qualification/primary-wallet-client.json). These exercise the actual local Beignet and native provider engines against Docker's regtest chain. They do not claim a rebuilt Umbrel package or production-network qualification.
